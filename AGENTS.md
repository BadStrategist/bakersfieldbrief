# BakersfieldBrief.com — overnight Kern County scanner pipeline

## Pipeline (kern_overnight)
`--phase1` early fetch+transcribe (03:00) → `--phase2` tail transcribe (05:20, 06:20) → CrimeRadar emit (06:50, 07:10) → write + publish (07:30, `ko_publish.py` backstop) → newsletter (08:10) → social (09:15). Full solo run: `.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m kern_overnight.run`.

- **Since 2026-10-15 the whole morning is ONE ordered runner**, not separate Hermes cron jobs: Windows task "BB Morning Runner" runs `C:\Users\tscac\bb-pipeline\runner.py` (02:40 + at sign-in; each time above is not-before, a late start runs the rest in order and resumes after a reboot). Windows task "BB Morning Report" (09:50) sends the only daily status message. The old Hermes BB morning jobs are paused; do not resume them or add new morning cron jobs. See `C:\Users\tscac\bb-pipeline\README.md`.

- The phase steps are **no_agent scripts** (`ko_phase1_cron.py`, `ko_phase2_cron.py` in `~/AppData/Local/hermes/scripts`), called by the runner. They spawn the project venv detached and wait. Each writes a `.lock` next to itself — never run two phases at once.
- **Never re-run fetch/extraction when a transcript already exists** — use `--extract-only`. Re-fetching with a transcript on disk caused a ~60-minute hang.
- Nothing auto-publishes. Drafts are delivered for human review.

## STT
faster-whisper `large-v3` on this PC (`KO_WHISPER_MODEL`, default `Systran/faster-whisper-large-v3`). The vocab priming cap is 224 tokens, and Whisper silently cuts overflow from the FRONT, so the highest-value terms go LAST in `vocab.txt`. Street names are verified against `kern_overnight/streets.txt` (6,275 entries).

## Parts of town
A part of town ("southwest Bakersfield") is a looked-up fact, never the writer's guess: the 2026-10-09 lead put Pembroke Avenue in the southwest, and it is north of the river. `kern_overnight/regions.py annotate incidents/<date>.json` sets a `region` on each incident it is sure of; the report names a part of town only from that field. `regions.py check <report> <incidents> --fix` corrects a claim the street contradicts. Unknown street, split street with no block number, or another town: no `region`, and the copy names none. The six parts and the lines between them are in `scripts/build_street_regions.py` (City of Bakersfield Streets layer, Census places, the Kern River); change a line there and rebuild `street_regions.json`.

## ALPR (/surveillance/)
One county page and its town pages (below). The Overpass diff runs in CI (`weekly.yml`, Monday) with `--alpr-change`: it appends the change-log row, advances the node snapshot, commits the artifacts, deploys, and fails loudly if the row for the Pacific date did not land. The local `~/AppData/Local/hermes/scripts/alpr_weekly.sh` is retired (it needed the box awake, and Overpass 406s from this machine). Never add a second Monday writer: the date guard in `append_change_log` refuses the twin row.

Scope: the camera list is Kern County, by the county's own OSM boundary (`KERN_AREA` in `build/sources/alpr.py`), never a box: until 2026-10-12 a box around the Bakersfield metro counted 270 under a heading that said Kern County, where the county had 455. The tag filter is `surveillance:type=ALPR` only; `manufacturer~Flock` brings in 68 Condor video domes that read no plates. The Bakersfield figures on the page are `metro_counts` over the same list. Every change-log row carries `scope`; the first weekly run after a scope change logs the cameras the wider map brought in as `widened`, and only cameras first mapped since the last row as new. A reply with no camera in a part of the county that held five or more on the last good list is a failed fetch (red run, page keeps the last good list).

Land ownership: a box job, never CI. `work/alpr_ownership/county_join.py all` joins every listed camera the join does not hold (resumable by node id; a failed call is never stored as an answer), `verify` is the acceptance check, then `rebuild_all.py` writes `data/alpr/`. Never run `merge_master.py` or `merge_contacts.py` again: they rebuild the master from scratch and erase `match_quality`, the district lines and the 81 hand-researched contact records. A camera is one of three things, counted from the data on every build and never typed: joined to an owner, looked up with no roll record to join (`norecord`: no titled parcel within 60 m, or a parcel the roll does not hold), or not looked up yet; the page gives no reason for the third. An owner with roll facts and no research carries `contact_status: not_researched`, and its record says the company and contact routes have not been looked up, never that none was found. A person's name is published only when the owner types as a company or an agency, so the doubtful name types as private.

Towns: which town a camera is in is a looked-up fact, the same rule as a part of town: its point is inside that city's or Census place's boundary (`build/places_kern.py` over `build/kern_places.json`, rebuilt by `scripts/build_kern_places.py`) or it has no town. 43 of 455 cameras stand within 15 m of a boundary, because city limits run down the roads the cameras are on; a camera past the line goes under "just outside", with the measured distance, and is not counted as the town's. `/surveillance/<town>/` (`build/builders/alpr_towns.py`) is built only for a town in `TOWNS` with eight or more cameras inside its places (ten to be listed), from ownership data that carries `norecord` (the sign of a county join) with 90 percent of the county's and the town's cameras looked up. Otherwise no page, no hub link, no sitemap entry, and the url redirects to the hub. Bakersfield has no town page: the hub is its page. A city below the floor, or with no mapped camera, gets a row and never a page: the hub's by-town table (`place_rows`) lists all eleven cities and every community holding a camera, and its column sums to the camera list.

Daily freshness: every `daily.yml` run ends with `scripts/check_alpr_fresh.py`, which fails the run and alerts ops Discord unless the camera list was fetched from Overpass on that run and is dated the Pacific date. The last-good-list fallback in `build/sources/alpr.py` stays (it keeps the page from showing zero cameras), but it returns `stale: True` for CI to read; `ok: True` there means "renderable", not "healthy". That flag never reaches a page, a card, an email or schema: the answer to stale data is a red run and an alert, never page copy. The daily run proves freshness only: it never writes the change log or `alpr_nodes.json`, so "newly mapped" keeps meaning "since last Monday". The Overpass query declares its budget (`[timeout:25][maxsize:33554432]`); without one a busy server refuses it with a 504. Fetch order: `overpass-api.de`, then its backends `lambert` and `gall`. A camera whose first-mapped date cannot be looked up is a `::warning` on a daily run and a red weekly run when it is one of that week's new ids (no row, snapshot not advanced). The land ownership join is manual (local box); the weekly run posts a reminder when it falls behind.

## Git (main carries data by design)
`main` carries source AND generated data. Two writers, disjoint paths:
- **CI owns** every path under `data/`, `email/` and `drafts/` EXCEPT the box-owned ones below. `scripts/ci_push.sh` stages by that exclusion, and aborts if a box-owned path reaches the index. It must be an exclusion list, not a fixed list of names: a whitelist silently stopped committing `data/fog_delays/log.json` and `data/last_guide.json` the day after it was written.
- **The box owns** `transcripts/`, `incidents/`, `data/overnight_reports/`, `data/geo_cache.json`, `data/incidents_geo.json`. Writers: `kern_overnight/transcribe.py` (transcripts), `ko_publish.py` (the nightly report and `incidents/<date>.json`), `kern_overnight/geo.py` (the geocode cache).

The box's checkout never commits: both box writers push from a worktree of origin/main (`ko_publish.py`, and `kern_overnight/boxpush.py` for the transcript checkpoints), and the 02:45 drift guard (`bb-pipeline/drift_guard.py`) resets the checkout to origin/main whenever every difference is already on origin. A local commit there is drift: in Oct 2026 checkpoint commits whose push lost the race with CI left the box 12 ahead / 24 behind.

Local work commits SOURCE ONLY; never `git add -A`. The five content workflows and `deploy.yml` share the `pages` concurrency group, so CI never races CI. To ship a source fix without the daily run (AI brief build, artifact commit, newsletter from 8 AM Pacific): put `[skip ci]` in the commit message, push, then `gh workflow run deploy.yml`. It builds `--no-llm`, runs the tests and deploys; it commits nothing and sends nothing. Because the two writers' paths are disjoint, a plain `pull --rebase` should never conflict. If one does, it is an ownership violation: stop and fix the ownership, do not auto-resolve. Scoped recovery for a local rebase conflict on a CI-owned path: `git rebase --abort`, `git checkout origin/main -- <that CI path>`, re-stage, commit source only. Never bulk-checkout the box-owned paths: that reverts an unpushed overnight report.

## CI alerts
Two alerts on a failed run, in this order: `scripts/notify_issue.py` comments on the pinned issue "Ops alerts: failed CI runs" (#1) with the run's own `GITHUB_TOKEN`, then the ops Discord step. The issue comment needs no secret, so it still arrives when `DISCORD_WEBHOOK_URL` is missing. It always exits 0 and carries no `continue-on-error`: it reports a red run and is never the reason for one. Keep the issue open and pinned; if it is closed the next failed run opens a new one.

## Locations on the report
An incident's `area` is an exact match in `kern_overnight/streets.txt` or it is empty. Never a fuzzy match, never a default city, and nothing at all when the text names another Kern town (`regions.names_another_town`). An empty area is correct output: the copy names no place.

The same rule one layer down, on the map: `geo.geocode` returns `None` for an empty area or one it cannot find, and `build_feed` leaves that row off (it logs how many). There is no city-centre fallback: 71 of the 115 dots on 2026-10-10 were that one point. The map's own street table matches whole words only ("18th Street" is not H Street).

For the app's History rows (`crimeradar_monitor._area_for`): a compass word the headline wrote is part of the name ("South Chester Avenue" is `S Chester Avenue` or nothing) and none is ever added; a feed card's place line counts only when it is one gazetteer street and the headline does not say otherwise; two streets in one headline is no area, logged. A town name is a street only when the region table holds that street (`Taft Highway` yes, `Grapevine Road` no). Change any of this and re-measure against every title in `work/crimeradar/` and every name in `streets.txt`, never a sample.

## Reel audio (scripts/brief_reel)
A dispatch clip is in the reel only when it verified: audible speech measured in the cut (1.5 s or more, and a quarter of its slot or more), a readable re-transcription of the cut, that text being the beat that was picked, and the clip measuring within 1 LU of -16 LUFS after its trim. A clip that fails is dropped and the reel is re-paced over the rest; it is never mixed. The 2026-10-12 reel mixed two clips of dead air and went out at -35.6 LUFS, bed only.

- A cut starts on a word's own time, from a probe transcribed with the voice detector on. Never on a Whisper segment stamp: a 30 s chunk that opens on silence comes back stamped 0.00 to 29.98.
- A window is the story's only when it carries an anchor: the street, the house or block number, or the words of the matched transcript row. "Fire", "medic" and the like pick nothing on their own (2026-10-09: a Pembroke Avenue shooting card played a brake fire).
- Levels are measured, never assumed (`reel_audio_gate.py`): each clip and the finished mix at -16 LUFS, the bed at -36 LUFS, peak under -1 dBTP. A mix that would need more than +12 dB has no voice in it and is refused. A clip's trim has the same cap, and a clip the cap leaves off -16 is dropped, never mixed quiet.
- The soundtrack is 1 s, one slot per clip, 4 s, and the picture is cut to the same plan. A cut shorter than its slot (the block ended, a short emulator clip) is padded to it in the mix, and its speech is judged against the slot. The written file's length is measured against the plan; `audio_gate` also holds a reel whose scene count is not its clip count or whose length is off the plan by more than 0.25 s.
- `make_dispatch_audio.py` exits 0 clean, 2 degraded (a story dropped), 4 held (no clip verified, no soundtrack written), 5 mix refused. `publish_social.py` asks `audio_gate` on the publish path every run: it reads the audit log AND measures `reel.mp4`. Held is exit 6, nothing posted, never a bed-only reel. Degraded publishes and exits 7. Neither is ever exit 0 or 3.
