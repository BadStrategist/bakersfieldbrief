# The Kern Overnight — August 24, 2026

A dispute at a wellness facility in southwest Bakersfield turned violent late Saturday night when a roommate reportedly poured hot water on another resident, bringing officers to the scene. Fire crews in the county were also kept busy with a brush fire that drew dozers and two battalions, and police handled a domestic call on Riverlakes Drive and a welfare check on Beardsley Avenue through the early hours.

## The big one

**Assault at a southwest Bakersfield wellness facility.** Just before 11 p.m., officers responded to a wellness facility on Evanston Avenue after a dispute escalated, with a roommate reportedly pouring hot water on another resident and on bedding and equipment at the scene.

## Around the county

**Brush fire draws dozers, unincorporated Kern County.** Around 9:20 p.m., a brush fire was reported in a field, possibly being put out by residents. Fire officials dispatched multiple engines, two battalions and dozers to the scene.

**Domestic disturbance on Riverlakes Drive.** Shortly after 11 p.m., officers responded to a domestic call in southwest Bakersfield in which residents were pushing and throwing property; a woman on scene said she was pregnant and had been struck.

**Welfare check on Beardsley Avenue.** Just after midnight, officers checked on a 19-year-old woman at a residence in southeast Bakersfield who was under the influence and had made suicidal statements.

## The log

**22:53** — A man with his face covered was reported in the Lake Isabella area, with a related report of someone tampering with property; units searched the area.

**01:22** — Units responded to a reported loitering and disturbance at a convenience store on 34th Street in central Bakersfield.

**03:26** — Fire crews answered a medical aid call in the Oshkosh Avenue area of southeast Bakersfield.

Overnight activity clustered on the southwest side of town, where a wellness-facility dispute and a domestic call drew police responses within the same hour, while the county brush fire was reported to be largely under control by residents before crews arrived.
