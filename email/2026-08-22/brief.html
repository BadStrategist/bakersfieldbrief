 The Kern Overnight

Scanner brief from 9pm to 6am

Overnight: shooting, Baker St

24 incidents tracked overnight · locations generalized  Full Report

The night’s most serious call came just after midnight, when a witness reported seeing someone shot at a Taco Bell in the Baker Street area. Officers responded with an officer-safety broadcast, and the suspect was described as a white male in his 40s to 50s, wearing a black shirt and dark pants, sitting inside a white vehicle — possibly a Ford Explorer. Earlier in the night, around 2:16 a.m., flames were visible inside a vacant single-story house on King Street with heavy smoke showing.

Multiple engines and a truck company responded, and the fire was under control by about 2:25. Just before 11 p.m., a two-vehicle collision left one vehicle on fire near Gosford Road and Harris Road. Engine 14 and Engine 9 responded — one car was smoking with another about a quarter mile back — and command dubbed it the Harris Incident.

Injuries were unknown in that incident. Multiple reports of a grass fire drew a large response — engines, patrol units, a water tender, a dozer and a helicopter — to southbound Highway 395 north of Brown Road. Crews found a small roadside fire that was quickly extinguished, and the extra equipment was canceled.

Around 10:38 p.m., an assault with a knife was reported with no injuries. The suspect — about 6 feet tall with neck tattoos, wearing black shorts, a gray shirt and white shoes — fled on foot northbound. Throughout the night, other calls included a subject reported with a firearm in his waistband on Houchin Road; an assault reported on Birch Avenue in Wasco; a hit-and-run on Monterey Street involving a black Camry and a white sedan; two fire hydrants reported spilling water on 22nd Street between Q and P streets; a small grass fire behind a residence on Windsong Street; a suspicious vehicle check on Culiacan Avenue; a family disturbance on Suncrest Drive involving an intoxicated teenager; a welfare check on a man lying on the ground near the curb on 26th Street; and an assault in progress with multiple people inside and a disturbance heard.

 Something Good

In a bit of positive news for our community, the Kern County Sheriff's Office has merged its emergency units to speed up water rescues in the county. This consolidation aims to improve response times and enhance rescue operations in water-related emergencies. The move comes as deputies have recorded eight rescues and five recovered persons from the Lower and Upper Kern River since the day after Memorial Day. A recent two-day training session at Pyles Boys Camp near Lake Ming gave volunteers hands-on practice in critical search and rescue tactics, including techniques for grabbing victims from the water and bringing them up into the air. By combining water and aerial support, rescuers now have multiple vantage points to more precisely locate someone in danger, potentially saving lives more quickly. This effort not only aims to save lives but also to teach the public what signs to watch for when someone may be in danger.
