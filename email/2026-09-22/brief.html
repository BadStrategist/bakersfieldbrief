 The Kern Overnight

Scanner brief from 9pm to 5am

What happened overnight: Armed Subject / Officer Safety at 1700 block Mount Vernon Ave

16 incidents tracked overnight · locations generalized  Full Report

The overnight hours brought a mix of serious calls and routine responses across Kern County, with deputies and fire crews staying busy from the east side of Bakersfield to the county line. Just after 11 p.m., a caller reported an assault with a baseball bat at the intersection of Flower Street and Lincoln Street, and officers were dispatched with a suspect description after the witness waited nearby for law enforcement. Earlier, late Sunday evening, a woman told dispatchers her husband had said he planned to kill himself, prompting deputies to track his location through a phone ping in the area east of Bakersfield, where units were checking the area.

On southbound Interstate 5 south of Bakersfield, a reported rollover drew a rescue, engine, truck, and battalion assignment alongside CHP, but the first crew on scene found the vehicle had rolled over and then left, canceling the rest of the response. Fire crews also responded to a gray SUV burning on Mount Vernon Avenue. The busiest stretch of the evening came between 9 and 11 p.m., when crews ran a gas leak at a home where the homeowner could not turn off the stove, checked the southbound side of Interstate 5 near the Grapevine for a vegetation fire that showed nothing, and answered a mutual aid request from Los Angeles County for a grass fire where Highway 138 meets Highway 14, sending an engine and a water tender.

Other calls included a disturbance on the 300 block of Chester Lane, a welfare check on the 2100 block of Coventry Drive, a traffic hazard on Highway 178 at Alfred Harrell Highway where three vehicles pulling jet skis were parked in westbound lanes, and a public assist on the 800 block of 8th Street. Deputies also cleared debris from Wilson Road after a roadway hazard report.

 Something Good

The AIS Cancer Center at Adventist Health hosted its 13th annual VIPink garden tea party event, a free community celebration honoring breast cancer survivors. The event, held at the cancer center on Chester Avenue, featured a survivor fashion show with patients as models, boutique shopping, hors d'oeuvres, and tea beverages from local vendors, bringing together survivors, family members, caregivers, and health professionals in a warm, supportive setting to mark a positive milestone for local cancer patients and survivors.
