 The Kern Overnight

Scanner brief from 9pm to 5am

What happened overnight: Structure Fire at Alley behind 1000 block Flower St at Owens S

17 incidents tracked overnight · locations generalized  Full Report

The garage fire came in at 2:21 a.m., when dispatchers sent a reinforced structure assignment to the 2200 block of C Street, across from a home there, after multiple callers reported fire on the same block. The first response included four engines, two truck companies, a rescue and two battalions. Crews held the address until a unit cleared the scene shortly after 3 a.m.

Up in Rosamond, crews were sent to a mobile home park on Rosamond Boulevard at 1:37 a.m. for several reports of a trailer on fire. The first medic squad on scene found a fully involved double-wide at the back of the park with no exposures — a vacant trailer that had been left outside the park on a graded lot. Crews knocked it down with the first tank of water and kept units on hand for water supply; a county engine and battalion added to the call were cancelled once things were under control.

Just before midnight, smoke brought a city and county battalion to the former Family Dollar building at Niles Street and Mount Vernon Avenue at 11:46 p.m. Crews found heavy smoke, and by 11:56 p.m. dispatchers confirmed primary and secondary searches were complete and the building was clear. Earlier, at 10:39 p.m., an engine responding to smoke near the freeway found a grass fire along the on-ramp from Highway 58 to Highway 99, then checked behind a nearby discount store and found nothing burning there.

At 12:59 a.m., an engine and an ambulance were sent to the gate of Kern Valley State Prison in Delano for a reported overdose, with the ambulance asked to stage in case transport was needed. On the police side, officers ran to disturbances — including a fight between two siblings — suspicious-person reports, a shots-heard call that put a description of a dark pickup leaving eastbound out to units, and a domestic violence report relayed from a hotel. There was also medical aid in the 300 block of Kentucky Street.

Police traffic thinned out after 3 a.m.

 Something Good

Nina Ha calls herself just another mother in Bakersfield, but the kids in this town know her as something else entirely. She started volunteering at her children's schools to read aloud, and one day, on a whim, she showed up dressed as Wonder Woman. The class loved it, and the invitations followed — Celebrity Reader Day, Read Across America, Career Day — and she's been doing it ever since.

Ha moved here more than three decades ago to start a family and found her community in the Bakersfield Chinese Women's Club, a group she says has been running for more than 75 years. Its biggest fundraiser is an annual Christmas dinner that raises thousands of dollars for hard-working high school students headed to college. Local restaurants have pitched in too — Ha said China Bistro has donated thousands of dollars in profits to support the club's fundraisers and student tuition. The costume is the fun part; the fund is the point.
