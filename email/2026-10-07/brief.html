 The Kern Overnight

Scanner brief from 9pm to 5am

What happened overnight: Collision at Southbound Hwy 99 north Lerdo Hwy

25 incidents tracked overnight · locations generalized  Full Report

The night's biggest call came just after 2 a.m., when crews were sent to a vehicle-versus-pedestrian crash on southbound Highway 99 just north of Lerdo Highway. A vehicle was reported facing the wrong way in the freeway, and as units responded, dispatchers learned three vehicles were involved and two people were hurt — one of them a pedestrian who was struck. That upgraded the call to a rescue.

The pedestrian was reported down in the number one lane and possibly in grave condition, and a second unit passing the scene about half a mile into the southbound lanes reported several vehicles involved. Elsewhere in east Kern County, a single vehicle landed on its roof on Highway 14, and a Mercy Air helicopter was placed on standby. Crews found one patient who was not pinned but had limited access, so they began an extrication; the air ambulance was later stood down.

Fire crews also answered smoke at the back of a commercial building in the 2100 block of Niles Street — a structure that had burned before — and found smoke at the rear on arrival. A car and a semi collided near East Bear Mountain Boulevard, with all occupants reported out of the vehicles, the car blocking traffic and one patient transported. On southbound Highway 99 south of Highway 166, a semi went into a guardrail with heavy smoke visible, hung up on the rail for about 50 feet; Caltrans was asked to respond to the road hazard.

The rest of the log was the usual overnight mix: a domestic disturbance on Gold Dust Drive, a semi with brakes on fire northbound on Interstate 5 north of the Grapevine whose driver pulled into the scales, an animal bite on East Roberts Lane, and a handful of medical aids, alarms and welfare checks across the county.

 Today's Weather

It's currently 75° at Meadows Field, on the way to a high near 100° this afternoon and a low around 70° tonight. No active weather alerts are in effect for Kern County, and there are no active fires reported. Air quality sits at 71 on the index — moderate — so if you're sensitive to smoke or dust, it's a day to keep an eye on how you're feeling during the hottest hours. Otherwise, expect a classic early-October valley scorcher: hot, dry and still. Drink more water than you think you need, and don't leave anyone in a parked car.

 Something Good

CSUB has hired Andy Fiske as its new athletic director, and the university is framing the move as a fresh chapter for Bakersfield's hometown university and its student-athletes. It's a hard moment to step into, and Fiske's stated first move — listening — is the right one. Here's hoping the Roadrunners get the steady, accountable leadership they deserve, on the field and off.
