 The Kern Overnight

Scanner brief from 9pm to 5am

What happened overnight: Armed Robbery at Union Ave

27 incidents tracked overnight · locations generalized  Full Report

Officers spent the small hours working a robbery on Union Avenue in which the weapon was a pocket knife. According to the overnight report, the suspects were described as a group of eight to ten people, and what was taken was food from the store. One vehicle, a newer red Hyundai, ran north on Union Avenue, and a second, an older white Hyundai, went south.

No arrests were reported. West of the oil fields near Fellows, fire crews answered a smoke report off Mid Oil and found roughly two to three acres burning in brush and grass. The fire, run under the name Kirby, was reported to have roots running on three sides, and several battalions, water tenders and a helicopter were assigned to it.

Earlier in the evening, a semi truck burned in a crash on the westbound side of Highway 58, and a truck caught fire on Grapevine Road just off the northbound Interstate 5 exit, closing the ramps there. Officers also went to the area of Lowe's on Columbus Street for shots heard — no one was hurt, and the call was being checked against a separate report of a suspicious person in the same parking lot. A caller reported a brandishing that had just happened at East California Avenue and Baker Street in east Bakersfield, near MLK Park, saying a man in a green shirt had a firearm and was running toward the caller, who was in a car.

The rest of the night was crashes, welfare checks, fights and medical aids stretching from Oildale out to North Edwards.

 Something Good

Saturday's Cultura Fest at Stramler Park was a good reminder of what this city does well — folklorico dancers, pottery and plants, food, and neighbors who came out to celebrate heritage while quietly funding scholarships for students who are the first in their families to go to college. The same spirit showed up at Allensworth, where families from across the state walked the grounds of a town built more than a century ago on the idea that Black families deserved to live free from discrimination and violence. Both events drew crowds on a warm October weekend, and both sent something forward — one toward tuition, the other toward memory.
