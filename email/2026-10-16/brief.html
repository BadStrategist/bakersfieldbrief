Deputies and firefighters had a long night, and the strangest call came out of Lamont. Two vehicles collided on East Bear Mountain Boulevard at General Beale Road, out in the rural stretch east of town, and the first units on scene found a large amount of blood on both cars and inside one of them — but nobody who needed an ambulance. The people involved said no one else had been riding with them, and deputies didn't trust that account.

They searched the wreckage and the ground around it for anyone who might have been thrown clear, and a sheriff's helicopter was called in to help sweep the area. The investigation stayed open. Fire carried the middle of the night.

A single-wide mobile home in the 200 block of Oswell Street was burning with moderate smoke showing when Battalion 4 arrived. Engine 41 was assigned to pull a line as fire attack, the rest of the assignment split between water supply and search, and crews reported a knockdown a few minutes later, cancelling a second battalion and a truck. Traffic was shut down in both directions on Oswell while hose lines lay across the road.

A second house fire drew a full assignment to the 1400 block of Ridgeview Street in east Bakersfield, and a report of a building on fire at 34th Street and San Dimas Street came in just before dawn. Elsewhere around the county: a woman swung an ax at a man in the 1400 block of Calcutta Drive in south Bakersfield and then left on foot still carrying it; a driver was found unresponsive behind the wheel at White Lane and Buena Vista Road with CPR under way; a man walked into his home in the 3400 block of Granada Avenue with blood on his hands; and a person was reported down on the railroad tracks on Truxtun Avenue approaching Baker Street, where police and medics worked both sides of the crossing while a train crew was asked how long the person had been there.

 Something Good

Vera Varner is 82 years old, and one night a year she gets to be a cheerleader again. Varner told 23ABC that Bakersfield College's homecoming is "almost magical" — a chance to reconnect with fellow alumni and perform at halftime, even if, as she joked, it's like a time machine "until the next day you're so sore you can't move." She's hoping more former BC cheerleaders join her at Saturday's game; she figures there should be at least 600 alumni out there. All you need is red and white and a signed waiver, and the station's story has the details.

Separately, KGET 17 reports the Sherna Armstrong Christmas Benefit Concert is looking for sponsors. If your business wants to help holiday music fill the air this season, that's an open door — and a good one.
