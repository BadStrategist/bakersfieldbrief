Read the full overnight log

The biggest call of the night came shortly after 1:15 a.m., when a commercial building near 30th Street and Union Avenue caught fire. Crews arrived to find the loading dock involved and power lines down along one corner of the structure. They set up a command post at the intersection and closed the northbound side of the road while they worked.

Firefighters knocked the flames down from the outside first, then moved in to finish off the interior fire, and reported the primary search clear. No injuries were reported. Fire carried the rest of the night too.

Multiple callers reported smoke showing from an apartment window in the 3400 block of Wible Road in southwest Bakersfield, where Truck 7, Truck 1 and two battalion companies were assigned to a second-floor unit in a two-story complex and knocked the fire down. A grass fire was also reported near Cottonwood and Bradshaw. Elsewhere, police ran a foot chase that ended near Lincoln and Robinson streets in east Bakersfield, with Air 1 overhead talking units onto one person detained on the 1200 block of Lincoln Street — officers were told the man came out of the passenger seat of a car that had been stopped and had tried to get into a house before he was caught.

A domestic call in the 7000 block of Willis Avenue turned up a report of a gun, and officers also worked an assault in which a caller said she was held down and had her phone taken. Two crashes drew responses: one at 18th and H streets that left vehicles blocking the roadway, and a single-car wreck on Mohawk Street that sent two people to the hospital by ambulance. A semi was reported driving the wrong way with bright headlights on Highway 58.

The log also shows a commercial fire alarm at a hotel on East Brundage Lane that crews traced without finding a fire, a pursuit in Arvin, and a report of a blue sedan stolen from a parking area.

 Something Good

The Via Arté chalk festival has turned a parking lot at the Marketplace on Ming Avenue into a walk-through gallery, with more than 100 artists at work. It's free and runs today and tomorrow from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m., with reservations available at viaartebakersfield.com — you can also buy a 2-by-2-foot square and make your own. Bakersfield Museum of Art marketing manager Lauren Marty told 23ABC the tradition reaches back to Italian street painters who made art on the pavement, something an everyday working person could actually see. Proceeds support the museum's exhibitions, art education and community programs. This year 22 high school teams are competing alongside featured artist Anitta Meza, who started in the student competition 13 years ago and is now creating a 12-by-15-foot piece inspired by words from her aunt, who is battling stage-four thyroid cancer — and it glows in the dark.
