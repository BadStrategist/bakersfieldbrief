Gunfire was reported twice overnight, in two different corners of the city. A little after 12:30 a.m., callers reported four to eight shots in the area of River Boulevard and Panorama Drive in east Bakersfield, and officers were sent to look. At midnight, two callers reported hearing two shots near Ming Avenue and Barrington Street in southwest Bakersfield.

Officers were also sent to a stabbing report near K Street at 4:32 a.m. Deputies responded to Harrington Street in southwest Bakersfield shortly after 1 a.m. on an assault with a deadly weapon report, where a man was reported swinging a metal pole at a woman. Nobody was hurt.

A second weapons call drew deputies to the 2nd and 3rd street area. A traffic stop on Fairfax Road turned into a pursuit that ran south on Madison Street and then through southeast Bakersfield on Watts, Lotus and Cottonwood before the driver pulled over westbound at Shellmacher Avenue before 8 p.m. Three people were in the car, and all were compliant.

Both stolen-vehicle calls came in the small hours — a Tahoe from a convenience store lot on Olive Drive, and a Kia from a delivery driver on Wilson Avenue in north Bakersfield. And the rain did real damage before 6 a.m., when part of a roof came down at an apartment on Actis Street in southwest Bakersfield and water ran inside the unit. Crews told the occupants to get out.

Elsewhere in the log: a caller asked officers to take an assault report on Washington Avenue in north Bakersfield, with the two people involved already gone in a gold sedan; Engine 41 was sent to a damaged power pole on Los Robles Drive; a man was found unconscious behind the wheel of a parked SUV at a gas station on Belvedere Avenue and an ambulance was called; an ambulance was called to Jackson Park for a person with traumatic injuries; crews handled a medical emergency on I Street in Mojave; and Engine 3 put out a trash can fire near the stadium.

 Today's Weather

It's 55 degrees at Meadows Field this morning, and we're looking at a high near 70 with a low around 55 tonight. The bigger story is the Flood Watch, issued by the National Weather Service office in Hanford at 5:38 a.m. and running until 8 p.m. tonight — so if you're out on the roads, give yourself extra room and watch for standing water in the usual low spots. Air quality is good, at an index of 33, and there are no active fires reported in the region. A cool, wet Thursday, then, with the kind of morning that makes the valley smell like rain.

 Something Good

The Hard Rock Casino Tejon's groundbreaking isn't just about a building — a 400-room hotel means housekeeping, food service, maintenance and front-desk jobs for people who live around Tejon and the Grapevine, plus new amenities for visitors passing through on I-5. It's the kind of project that ripples outward into the local economy for years. Worth a nod this morning.

And Friday brings the Hot Pink Celebration, hosted by the local nonprofit Links for Life at Luigi's, with doors opening at 6 p.m. The evening brings together patients, survivors, families and healthcare professionals for Breast Cancer Awareness Month. Links for Life provides wigs, grocery assistance and support groups to patients and survivors here in Kern County. As executive director Jennifer Henry told 23ABC, roughly one in eight women will be diagnosed with breast cancer, and about 450 women in Kern County will hear that news this year. Adventist Health nurse supervisor Jacqui Engstrom noted that only about 65 percent of local women are getting their screenings — a reminder that a monthly self-exam and, for women over 40, a yearly mammogram, are the simplest tools we have.
