The night's most serious call ended without anyone getting hurt. Officers responded to a report of a man holding a knife in his left hand who would not cooperate with commands. He wasn't advancing on anyone, but he stayed armed and refused to put the weapon down, so supervisors set up command and held the scene instead of moving in.

About 35 minutes later, officers closed in and took him into custody. Charges including assault with a deadly weapon, criminal threats and child endangerment were requested. Earlier in the evening, airport police cleared the terminal at Meadows Field after a report of a possible firearm on the property.

A man in a black hoodie and black shorts was seen heading toward the employee parking area below the front of the terminal. Officers held the terminal while they searched and reported all channels clear within a few minutes. Elsewhere around the county, a caller on Pond Road reported about five people firing guns into the air, with three vehicles described leaving the area — a gray Volkswagen, a black truck and a red Chevrolet.

Crews went to Bliss and Texas streets for a mini-bike against a vehicle with possibly two patients; Engine 6 found one person down in the roadway and staged incoming units while medics went to work. On Center Street in east Bakersfield, a caller reported a man hitting windows with a machete he kept in his vehicle; he was described as 35 years old and believed to be under the influence of alcohol. Fires kept crews moving all night — a U-Haul on Lake Street, a vehicle on Mayer Avenue, a patch of grass and brush larger than a car near Alfred Harrell Highway, and a structure fire call after 4 a.m.

The log also included a pair of court order calls that turned up no violations, a two-vehicle crash that left the roadway blocked, a Nissan crash on Harris Road, an assault on California Avenue involving a victim in a wheelchair, and a suspected DUI driver who tried to run from a crash before witnesses held him on the sidewalk.

 Today's Weather

 Something Good

Honor Flight Kern County's return to Meadows Field was the kind of homecoming worth pausing for — veterans stepping off the plane to friends, family and community members who turned out to welcome them back. For Lowell Tallon, the trip doubled as a family reunion in the capital, with both his son and daughter alongside him at the memorials. After a cancellation that stung the whole program, this one landed exactly the way it should.
