It was a busy night across the county, starting with two weapons calls that came in within a couple of hours of each other. Around 8:17 p.m., officers were sent to Worthington Avenue and Lowell Drive in north Bakersfield for a woman reported to be hitting vehicles with a baton-style stun gun and screaming; she was described as wearing a black hoodie and dark pants. A little over two hours later, at 10:29 p.m., deputies headed to the Love's truck stop on Boron Frontage Road North for a man reported to be walking through the parking lot with a sword.

Earlier in the evening, just after 8 p.m., officers took a secondhand report of two men in masks and all black chasing a woman in the 6200 block of Brahma Street in south Bakersfield. Domestic violence calls came in through the night from an apartment in the 2700 block of North Chester Avenue, from Baker Street in east Bakersfield, from Beardsley Avenue, from the 3400 block of Anderson Street in southeast Bakersfield, and from one address where the man involved was already the restrained party on a served protective order. Just after midnight, officers were sent to a cell phone store at Niles Street and Glenwood Avenue, where a man who reportedly walked out without paying for phones was chased by other customers; the call came in as an attempted robbery.

Around 1:20 a.m., officers stopped a car and several people ran, and a perimeter was set before two of them were caught. A traffic stop just before 2 a.m. turned into a pursuit that ran eastbound at 42 mph and then north at 18 mph before dead-ending into a cul-de-sac near the 1300 block of Chamberlain Avenue in east Bakersfield, where one person was taken into custody. And at 3:37 a.m., deputies went to a Union Pacific train at Comanche and Edison after the crew reported someone had cut the train's air flow regulator, forcing an emergency stop.

 Today's Weather

It's 57 degrees at Meadows Field this morning, and we're headed to a high near 69 with a low around 53 tonight — a mild, easy fall Friday. There are no active weather alerts for Kern County, and the air quality index is sitting at 39, which is good. No active fires are being reported in the county. After Thursday's rain — more on that below — today looks like the kind of afternoon worth taking outside.

 Something Good

There's genuinely encouraging news on the health front: Kern County has recorded zero fentanyl-related child deaths for two years, according to a new report. That's a milestone worth pausing on — it reflects real, sustained work by the people trying to keep this drug away from kids, and it's a meaningful sign for the safety of local families. Whatever is being done right, it's worth keeping up.

And in McFarland, the homecoming story is its own small bright spot — a town that lost its parade two days before the big week and turned a gym full of bleachers into something the younger kids will remember anyway.
