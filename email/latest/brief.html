What happened overnight: Structure fire at Mount Vernon Ave near Niles St

9 incidents tracked overnight · locations generalized  Full Report

Fire crews had a busy night across the county, starting with a structure fire that broke out near Mount Vernon Avenue and Niles Street on the east side of Bakersfield just before 1 a.m. A passerby spotted smoke coming from a building, and the call brought a heavy response — multiple engines, two trucks and battalion chiefs. Crews knocked the fire down within about 15 minutes, completed a primary search and stayed on scene for roughly half an hour before clearing.

No injuries were reported. Just before dawn, a vehicle fire sparked in a hotel parking lot on Marriott Drive. A caller reported that a car had struck a parked vehicle, which then caught fire, and a second caller feared someone might still be inside the burning car.

Fire crews and an ambulance were sent to the scene as a precaution. On the other side of town, a grass fire in the Hageman Road area smoldered with light smoke overnight. Crews said it was contained to an irrigation area with no immediate threat to structures.

Firefighters made contact with the landowner and planned to return this morning with heavy equipment to make sure it was fully out. Deputies also handled two possible domestic incidents overnight. The first was reported in the Paramount Colony area late Monday, where a caller said the driver of a dark sedan holding a toddler had left an In-N-Out parking lot while a domestic disturbance was in progress.

The vehicle’s direction remained unknown, and deputies responded to the area. The second was called in before dawn at an apartment complex on White Lane, after a neighbor heard a woman screaming and a man shouting. Dispatchers kept an open line with a woman who was crying and treated the call as a possible domestic incident in progress.

 Something Good

There was real warmth to be found at Stramler Park over the holiday weekend. Hundreds of Kern County workers and their families gathered for the annual Labor Day picnic, a day meant to celebrate the people who keep this valley running — the nurses, the builders, the teachers, the folks who show up every day. Between the food and the festivities, union members brought their grandchildren and their stories, carrying on a tradition that stretches back generations. As Sandy Reding put it, the benefits workers enjoy today weren’t handed out; they were won, and the next generation was there to see it. It’s a reminder that even as technology changes the workplace, the people doing the work deserve to be seen.
