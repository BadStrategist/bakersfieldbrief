It was a busy night for Kern County dispatchers, who logged 27 incidents between 7:04 p.m. and 6:42 a.m. Fires accounted for the largest share — 12 in all — with five medical or overdose calls, three reports of suspicious activity, two welfare and disturbance calls, and one commercial alarm. The evening opened with a structure fire reported just after 7 p.m., followed within the half hour by a vehicle fire and a separate fire call.

Around 8:15 p.m. crews were sent to a vehicle-versus-pedestrian incident, and a commercial fire alarm came in a few minutes later, alongside an outside fire. A fire near Truxtun Avenue was logged at 9:12 p.m., and another fire call came in just after 10 p.m. An officer assist and a probation check rounded out the late-evening entries.

Overnight, dispatchers handled medical aid calls at 12:16 a.m., 3:34 a.m., and 5:53 a.m., and three suspicious-person reports spread between 10:59 p.m. and 1:19 a.m. A welfare call near Jefferson Street came in at 1:58 a.m. Fire crews were dispatched again at 2:56 a.m., to a grass fire at 3:09 a.m., and to another fire at 3:54 a.m.

A burglary was reported at 5:07 a.m., and the log closed with an outside fire near Golden State Avenue at 6:15 a.m. and a vehicle fire at 6:42 a.m. A reminder that these entries come from preliminary dispatch reports and are generalized to protect privacy; details stand until an official source confirms them.

 Today's Weather

It's 63 degrees at Meadows Field as of this morning, on the way to a high near 81 and a low around 55 tonight — a genuinely warm November day. The National Weather Service in Hanford has issued a Lake Wind Advisory that took effect at 9:33 a.m. and runs through 10 a.m. Saturday, so boaters and anyone working outside should plan for gusty conditions over the next few days. Air quality is 81, which registers as moderate; those with sensitivities may want to keep an eye on it as the day warms. No active fires were reported in the region.

 Something Good

The Giving Machine is coming back to Bakersfield for a second year, and that's worth a smile. The big red vending machine lets people buy essential goods and services for neighbors in need rather than for themselves. Five local nonprofits will benefit this year — CAPK Food Bank, Golden Empire Gleaners, The Salvation Army, Flood Ministries, and Catholic Charities of Bakersfield. Last year's machine raised roughly $80,000 for local groups, and organizers say 100 percent of donations go straight to the participating nonprofits. City lead Michelle Lord told 23ABC that Bakersfield was chosen because it's "such a caring community," and that the goal is simple: make the holidays meaningful for neighbors here at home. Organizers are also planning community performances and family activities around the machine's run.
