 The Kern Overnight

Scanner brief from 9pm to 5am

What happened overnight: Multi-vehicle Crash at northbound Interstate 5

38 incidents tracked overnight · locations generalized  Full Report

The night's biggest call came early, just after 2:20 a.m., when drivers on both sides of northbound Interstate 5 about two miles south of Taft Highway started dialing in about a crash. The first crew in found one vehicle fully involved in flames and a second off the shoulder, with early reports putting as many as four vehicles in the wreckage and one car left sitting in the center divider. A second ambulance came in from Lamont while firefighters knocked the fire down and units kept working the roadway.

Earlier in the evening, just after 7 p.m., responders were called to a multi-vehicle crash elsewhere in the county where two people were pinned inside a single car — one critical, one moderate. The occupants of the other vehicles were already gone by the time units arrived, and crews called for an extra engine to cut the two patients free. Shortly before midnight, officers put out an officer safety broadcast after CHP passed along a report of a man with a silver rifle near Highway 99 at Ming Avenue, tied to a white Honda SUV and only about five minutes old.

Just before 9 p.m., police and medics went to an apartment at the highest priority for a young child reported not breathing after what was described as a possible bathtub accident, with an engine and ambulance responding together. And a caller on north Chester Avenue reported being assaulted with a firearm behind a business — a man with a handgun had fired it, no one was hurt, and the people who called had left before officers arrived. The rest of the log was grass and vehicle fires from the valley floor out to the eastern county, plus alarms, welfare checks and medical aids.

 Today's Weather

It's 72 degrees right now at Meadows Field, on the way to a high near 86 and a low around 61 tonight. No active weather alerts are posted for Kern County, and air quality is running moderate at 68 — fine for most folks, though sensitive readers may want to keep an eye on it if you're planning a long afternoon outside. Fire crews remain on one active fire in Kern, the Cyrus Fire, so the dry grass and the warm afternoon are worth respecting if you're out in the hills or working equipment near open ground.

 Something Good

The new veterans center headed for southwest Bakersfield is worth a smile this morning. For local service members and their families, it means care and support closer to home instead of a long drive out of the county — a real step toward making sure the people who served us don't have to travel far to be looked after. It's the kind of quiet, practical investment that pays off for years.
